# Evaluation goal

Evaluate whether an item gives an AI student accurate insight into how law, intellectual property, and public institutions interact with AI and digital systems.

# Scoring rubric

- **9-10: Material legal change or authoritative decision.** A statute, regulation, judicial interpretation, landmark judgment, enforcement decision, or official standard with concrete scope and consequences.
- **7-8: Important development.** A credible draft, consultation, regulator guidance, digital-government initiative, public-sector AI project, or well-evidenced legal analysis that could change compliance, rights, research, or public administration.
- **5-6: Useful background.** A clear explainer, incremental case, or policy discussion that improves understanding of an AI/law/public-governance intersection but does not represent a current binding change.
- **3-4: Low priority.** Commentary without a named source, narrow market commentary, or legal news with little relevance to technology or public policy.
- **0-2: Noise.** Advertising, rumor, copied content, political outrage without evidence, or unsupported legal conclusions.

# Evaluation guidance

Prioritize the type of legal instrument or public project, issuing authority, jurisdiction, procedural stage, effective date, concrete scope, and the technology involved. Clearly distinguish enacted law, draft text, consultation, proposal, guidance, judgment, pilot project, procurement plan, and media interpretation. Do not infer that a proposal is law, that a pilot is deployed, or that one case creates a general rule. Penalize generic legal or political news with no meaningful AI, data, digital-government, or IP connection.

Use three to five specific tags covering jurisdiction, legal domain, and technology topic.
