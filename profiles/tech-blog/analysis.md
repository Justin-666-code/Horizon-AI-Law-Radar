# Evaluation goal

Evaluate whether a technology article improves an AI undergraduate's ability to
judge or build a product: define a real user problem, choose between model and
deterministic approaches, set acceptance criteria, design fallback, estimate
cost, or ship a maintainable system. Score the article itself, not the
reputation of its author or publisher.

# Scoring rubric

- **9-10: Exceptional.** Makes a difficult product tradeoff concrete with strong
  evidence, working experience, or measurable results, and leaves the reader
  with a reusable requirement, evaluation method, cost model, or failure-handling
  pattern.
- **7-8: High value.** Provides substantial technical depth and connects it to a
  real deliverable, user workflow, integration constraint, or operational
  tradeoff.
- **5-6: Useful.** Explains a real technology competently but remains mostly
  implementation detail, is lightly evidenced, or does not yet show why the
  capability is worth delivering.
- **3-4: Shallow.** Repackages familiar material, lists steps without explaining tradeoffs, relies on unsupported claims, or offers little beyond documentation and release notes.
- **0-2: Noise.** Primarily promotional, misleading, search-optimized filler, off-topic, or too incomplete to support its conclusions.

# Evaluation guidance

Consider the clarity of the motivating problem, strength of the product
judgment, technical depth, evidence quality, honesty about limitations, and
whether a second-year student can extract one realistic next step. Reward
explicit acceptance criteria, cost, reliability, fallback, permissions, and
integration constraints. Penalize demo-only claims, hype, and prestige. Do not
reward length or novelty by itself.

Use three to five specific topic tags.
