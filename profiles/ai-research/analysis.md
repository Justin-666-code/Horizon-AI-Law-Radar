# Evaluation goal

Evaluate whether a research item can help an AI undergraduate understand a
frontier problem, capability boundary, evaluation method, or credible empirical
finding that changes technical judgment.

# Scoring rubric

- **9-10: Foundational or unusually actionable.** A new result that changes how a major problem is approached, provides unusually strong evidence, or introduces a method an undergraduate could study and reproduce.
- **7-8: High value.** A clear research contribution with a sound method, meaningful baselines, useful data, or a practical insight that connects to current AI systems.
- **5-6: Useful context.** Incremental research, a useful benchmark, or a credible survey that is worth knowing but does not change near-term understanding.
- **3-4: Low priority.** Narrow incremental work with weak evidence, an uninformative abstract, or a paper whose practical relevance is unclear.
- **0-2: Noise.** Marketing, unsupported claims, off-topic material, or content with no identifiable research contribution.

# Evaluation guidance

Reward a precise problem statement, methodological clarity, reproducible
details, appropriate baselines, honest limitations, and relevance to a product
or system decision. Penalize work that cannot be explained to a second-year
beginner or that assumes several unexplained advanced prerequisites. Do not
reward prestige, acronyms, benchmark saturation, model scale, or a long list of
results by themselves. Distinguish peer review, preprint status, and vendor
claims when the source provides that information.

Use three to five specific technical tags.
